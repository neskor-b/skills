# CV Review Rubric

Use evidence, not taste. Rate every dimension `Strong`, `Adequate`, `Needs work`, or `Critical`. `Critical` means a material application risk, not a judgment of the candidate.

## Rating anchors

| Dimension | Strong | Adequate | Needs work | Critical |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Target fit | Role, seniority, and vacancy priorities are immediately supported by relevant evidence. | The target is clear and most central requirements are covered. | The target is broad or strong evidence is buried. | The CV targets the wrong role or makes the candidate's fit impossible to determine. |
| Evidence | Important skills are demonstrated in Experience or Projects. | Most core skills have at least one credible example. | Many skills are listed without application context. | Central claims are unsupported, contradictory, or apparently fabricated. |
| Impact | Bullets show confirmed outcomes, scale, or honest proxies. | Several bullets communicate useful outcomes. | Most bullets describe responsibilities only. | The CV invents metrics or relies on unverifiable superlatives. |
| Technical depth | Architecture, complexity, constraints, and trade-offs are clear where relevant. | Technologies and substantive technical work are understandable. | Tool names dominate while engineering contribution remains vague. | The technical content is misleading or incompatible with the claimed role. |
| Ownership | Personal decisions, contribution, mentoring, and leadership are accurately distinguished. | The candidate's role is usually understandable. | Team outcomes repeatedly obscure individual contribution. | The CV converts team work into false sole authorship or inflates leadership. |
| Clarity | Concise, specific, well ordered, and easy to scan. | Generally clear with limited redundancy or vague wording. | Repetition, jargon, dense paragraphs, or generic language slows scanning. | Major information is missing, disorganized, or difficult to interpret. |
| ATS parseability | One column, standard headings, linear order, consistent dates, and text-based content. | Mostly safe with minor formatting risks. | Several layout or naming choices may impair parsing. | Image-only content, complex columns, tables, text boxes, or broken reading order hide material facts. |
| Credibility | Dates, titles, claims, and metrics are consistent and proportionate. | No material contradiction is apparent. | Ambiguous dates, titles, levels, or imprecise claims require confirmation. | Material facts conflict or appear knowingly false. |
| Consistency | Tense, date style, names, punctuation, and formatting are coherent. | Minor inconsistencies do not obstruct reading. | Frequent inconsistencies reduce polish or clarity. | Conflicts make chronology or identity unreliable. |
| Remote readiness | Relevant English communication and distributed-work evidence are demonstrated when available. | The document is professionally written for an international audience. | International positioning is generic or unsupported. | Language or location claims are misleading, or the CV is not usable for the stated market. |

Do not convert these ratings into a claimed ATS probability. If a numeric value is useful internally for ordering issues, do not expose it as an application prediction.

## Severity and recommendations

Order findings by material effect:

1. False, contradictory, or unsupported claims.
2. Missing target-critical qualifications or evidence.
3. Unclear ownership and impact.
4. Parsing and chronology risks.
5. Clarity, relevance, consistency, and polish.

For each recommended change:

- identify the exact section or wording;
- explain why it weakens credibility, relevance, evidence, or readability;
- state the concrete correction;
- distinguish a rewrite supported by existing facts from a question that needs new evidence.

Preserve strengths. Name existing evidence that should remain or be made more visible.

## REVIEW response contract

Return these sections in this order:

```markdown
# CV Review

## Verdict
A concise assessment of readiness and the largest material risk.

## Critical issues
Only the highest-impact blockers and credibility risks.

## Strong evidence
Specific content that already supports the target role.

## Review rubric
All ten dimensions with rating and brief evidence.

## Recommended changes
Prioritized, concrete corrections.

## Missing evidence
The single next question, or a short list only when the user explicitly requested a complete audit rather than an interactive review.
```

Do not provide a replacement CV in `REVIEW` unless the user requests rewriting.
