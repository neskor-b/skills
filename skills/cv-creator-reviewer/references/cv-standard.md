# International IT CV Standard

Use this standard for international remote technical roles. Adapt it when a vacancy or local market explicitly requires something different.

## Core principle

Make the CV a concise set of evidence that the candidate can perform the target role. Optimize for fast human scanning and reliable text parsing. Treat ATS compatibility as parseability plus truthful vacancy alignment, never as a guaranteed pass or universal score.

## Structure and ordering

Use one column and reverse chronology. Select only sections that add evidence:

1. Name and target role.
2. Email, phone, location or time zone.
3. Relevant LinkedIn, GitHub, portfolio, or personal site.
4. Optional Professional Summary.
5. Technical Skills grouped by useful categories.
6. Professional Experience.
7. Optional Selected Projects.
8. Education.
9. Optional certifications, publications, talks, or awards.

For junior candidates, move Education or Projects upward when they provide stronger evidence. For experienced candidates, lead with Experience. Give certifications more prominence only when meaningful to the target role. Omit a Summary that merely repeats Skills and Experience.

Prefer one page for junior and early-middle candidates when evidence fits without crowding. Use up to two readable pages for experienced middle, senior, staff, or management candidates. Relevance and legibility outweigh a mechanical page limit.

## Evidence-rich content

Write experience bullets using the strongest confirmed version of:

`accurate action + individual contribution + technical context + scale + outcome`

Prioritize:

- systems, products, and user or business problems;
- the candidate's specific contribution and decisions;
- architecture, constraints, and trade-offs when relevant;
- reliability, performance, cost, delivery, security, adoption, or product outcomes;
- collaboration, mentoring, and leadership demonstrated through actions.

Use exact metrics only when confirmed. When no exact metric exists, use an honest confirmed proxy such as:

- teams, services, regions, users, or workloads served;
- production adoption;
- ownership of a critical component;
- a manual step removed;
- an incident class eliminated;
- a new capability enabled;
- delivery or review responsibility.

Do not pressure the candidate into inventing numbers.

## Technical skills

Group skills into compact categories such as Languages, Frameworks, Databases, Cloud & Infrastructure, Testing, Observability, Security, and Tools. Put the most relevant categories first.

- List only actual skills.
- Support important skills in Experience or Projects where possible.
- Do not use bars, stars, percentages, or arbitrary proficiency labels.
- Do not put suggested technologies or placeholders in the clean CV.
- Demonstrate soft skills through evidence rather than labels such as “team player.”

## Vacancy alignment

Map required and preferred qualifications before tailoring. Classify each as:

- confirmed match;
- partial or transferable match;
- genuine gap;
- unsupported claim.

Reuse vacancy language naturally when it accurately describes confirmed experience. Reorder and select evidence to highlight the closest fit. Never use keyword stuffing, rewrite an official title deceptively, or present a learning goal as current professional experience.

## ATS-safe presentation

Use:

- standard headings such as `Professional Experience`, `Technical Skills`, `Projects`, and `Education`;
- a linear reading order;
- ordinary bullets;
- consistent month/year or year date formats;
- a text-based PDF or DOCX when the application allows it;
- contact information in the document body.

Avoid:

- columns, tables, text boxes, graphics, photographs, and word art;
- important text in headers or footers;
- image-only PDF files;
- stylized bullets and broken words across lines;
- decorative skill charts or icons replacing text;
- unclear abbreviations when the full term matters.

When PDF is requested, extract its text and verify order and completeness. Parsing systems differ and can be inaccurate; never label a document “ATS-proof.”

## Language and credibility

Use specific, active, factual English. Remove personal pronouns, clichés, vague intensifiers, internal jargon, and repetitive AI-like sentence patterns. Prefer clarity over inflated language.

Do not add:

- date of birth, gender, marital status, full home address, health, beliefs, passport data, references, or salary expectations unless relevant and explicitly requested;
- a photograph unless the target market or employer explicitly expects it;
- any confidential employer data the candidate is not allowed to disclose.

## Research sources

- [MIT: Make your resume ATS-friendly](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/make-your-resume-ats-friendly/) — simple formatting, meaningful vacancy keywords, supported file types, and plain-text testing.
- [MIT: Resume checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/) — readable typography, margins, consistency, and contextual page length.
- [Harvard: Guide to creating a strong resume](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/) — active, specific, fact-based, quantified, skimmable content and reverse chronology.
- [LinkedIn: Future of Recruiting 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/future-of-recruiting-2025) — the current shift toward skills-first hiring and evidence of skills.
- [Greenhouse: Unsuccessful resume parse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse) — documented parsing problems with columns, tables, graphics, headers, footers, and text boxes.
- [Greenhouse: Supported candidate upload formats](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360052218132-Supported-formats-for-resumes-cover-letters-and-other-candidate-uploads) — supported resume file types.
- [SAP SuccessFactors: Configuring resume parsing](https://help.sap.com/docs/successfactors-recruiting/setting-up-and-maintaining-sap-successfactors-recruiting/configuring-resume-parsing) — parsing is not always fully accurate.
- [Europass: Profile information guidance](https://europass.europa.eu/en/what-type-information-should-i-include-my-europass-profile) — omit sensitive information irrelevant to career and training.
